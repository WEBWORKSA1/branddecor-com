Glass partitions, storefront windows and doors are prime real estate. They're seen from both sides, they're at eye level, and film is one of the most affordable branding materials there is. The right film can add privacy, meet safety requirements and advertise — all in one job.

## The main types of film

**Frosted (etch-effect) film.** Mimics sandblasted glass. Used solid for privacy, or with your logo or pattern cut out so the clear areas form the design. The classic choice for meeting rooms and office fronts.

**Manifestation film.** Markings applied to glass walls and doors so people notice the glass and don't walk into it. Many building codes and accessibility standards require visible markings on large glazed areas, typically as bands within a set height range above the floor. It doesn't have to be dull dots — a branded band or logo pattern can satisfy the requirement if it's visible enough. Check your local code for exact heights and contrast.

**Printed storefront vinyl.** Full-colour graphics for windows: promotions, product imagery, hours and logos. Can be permanent adhesive or removable static cling for seasonal campaigns.

**Perforated window film (one-way vision).** Printed on the outside, with tiny holes that let people inside see out. Popular for storefronts and vehicle windows. Typical perforation patterns leave around half the film open, so images should be bold and simple.

**Gradient and patterned film.** Fades from frosted to clear, giving privacy at seated height while keeping the space open above. Great for open-plan offices.

**Solar and safety film.** Reduces heat and glare or holds shattered glass together. Not decorative on its own, but can be combined with decorative layers.

## Typical costs

| Film | Typical installed range (per sq ft) | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Plain frosted privacy | $8 – $14 | Meeting rooms, bathrooms |
| Frosted with cut logo / pattern | $12 – $20 | Office fronts, manifestation |
| Printed storefront vinyl | $10 – $20 | Retail and restaurant windows |
| Perforated one-way | $10 – $18 | Storefronts wanting outward visibility |
| Solar / heat control | $8 – $15 | Sunny façades |

Small jobs usually carry a minimum installation charge. Price your panes with the [window film calculator](/tools.html#t-film).

## Design rules for glass

1. **Design for both sides.** Frosted film looks the same from both sides; printed film usually doesn't. Decide which side matters most.
2. **Keep sightlines.** In retail, don't cover more than you need — people want to see inside. Many brands keep the eye-level zone mostly clear and brand the top and bottom bands.
3. **Contrast wins.** White or frosted elements read best on glass with a darker interior behind; dark graphics read best against bright interiors.
4. **Respect the frame.** Leave a small, even margin from mullions and frames rather than running graphics to the edge.
5. **Check local rules.** Some cities limit how much of a storefront window can be covered by signage. Ask your installer.

## DIY or pro?

Plain frosted film on small, flat panes is a reasonable DIY job with patience, a spray bottle of slip solution and a good squeegee. Cut logos, large panes, printed graphics and anything requiring perfect alignment across multiple panes are worth a professional. Bubbles, dust specks and misaligned seams are very visible on glass.

<div class="callout"><b>Seasonal tip:</b> Use static-cling vinyl for campaigns you'll change every few weeks. It goes on and comes off without residue and can often be reused next year.</div>
