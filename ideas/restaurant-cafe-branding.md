Guests don't only taste a restaurant — they photograph it, share it and use those images to decide where to go next. That makes interior branding one of the most cost-effective marketing investments a hospitality business can make. The goal isn't to put your logo everywhere; it's to create a few memorable moments that are unmistakably yours.

## Design one photo moment

Find the wall that already appears in guest photos — look at your tagged posts — or create one deliberately. Good candidates are visible from many seats, well lit and roughly 8 feet wide so a group fits in frame. Popular approaches:

- **LED neon slogan** — a short, witty line in your brand voice. Neon flex is far more durable and efficient than glass neon and photographs beautifully.
- **Bold mural** — a pattern, illustration or oversized food photography.
- **Living wall** — preserved moss or greenery with a cut-out logo; needs no watering if preserved.
- **Texture** — tiled, panelled or limewashed walls that give photos depth.

Keep your handle or logo subtle within the moment. Guests share it because it looks good, not because it's an ad.

## Menu boards and wayfinding

Menu boards are signage people read for minutes, not seconds. Keep the hierarchy clear: categories, items, then prices. Use your brand typeface and enough contrast to read from the queue — about 1 inch of letter height per 10 feet of distance is a good start. Digital menu boards make price changes and day-parting easy; chalkboards suit craft brands. Don't forget clear signs for washrooms, ordering and pickup — confusion is a poor experience.

## Windows and patio

Window vinyl with your logo, hours and a single offer turns walkers into guests. A projecting or blade sign is visible from down the street. On patios, branded umbrellas, planters and A-frame boards extend your identity onto the sidewalk — check local permits for anything on public space.

## The details guests remember

- **Washrooms:** a surprising wallpaper or a funny sign in a washroom is one of the most photographed spots in many restaurants.
- **Tableware and menus:** a stamp, embossing or custom napkins carry the brand into every photo of food.
- **Staff areas visible to guests:** open kitchens and bars are part of the stage.
- **Lighting:** warm, dimmable lighting flatters food and faces. Light your photo wall separately.

## Materials that survive hospitality

Kitchens, bars and dining rooms are hard on finishes. Choose cleanable, scuff-resistant wallcovering over basic vinyl in high-traffic zones, seal wood, keep neon and electrical items away from splash zones, and laminate printed graphics. Ask installers about grease, steam and fire-rating requirements for your space.

## A sample budget split

| Item | Share of a small café's branding budget |
|---|---|
| Exterior sign and window vinyl | 30–40% |
| Photo-moment wall (neon or mural) | 20–30% |
| Menu boards and interior signage | 15–20% |
| Small touches (washroom, tableware, stickers) | 10–15% |
| Lighting adjustments | 5–10% |
