A mural is the fastest way to transform a large, plain wall into something people photograph and remember. Businesses use them to tell origin stories, show off products, display values, create an Instagram moment or simply make a space feel alive. The first decision — printed or painted — shapes everything else.

## Four ways to make a business mural

### Printed adhesive vinyl
Your design printed on wall-grade adhesive vinyl, usually laminated, and applied in panels. It's quick to install, available in removable versions for leased spaces and very consistent in colour. It needs a smooth, cured, painted wall; heavy texture and fresh paint are its enemies.

### Printed wallcovering
Commercial wallcovering (often called Type II) printed digitally and hung with paste like wallpaper. It's tougher and more scuff-resistant than vinyl, handles light texture better and is the go-to for corridors, hospitality and healthcare. It's more permanent — removal usually means some wall repair.

### Printed fabric (tension or SEG)
Dye-sublimated fabric stretched into an aluminium frame. No adhesive touches the wall, graphics can be swapped in minutes, and fabric can be backlit for a glowing effect. Popular for retail and offices that refresh campaigns often.

### Hand-painted mural
An artist paints directly on the wall. Nothing beats it for texture, scale on rough surfaces (brick, block, exterior) and originality, and it can become a local landmark. It takes longer, depends heavily on the artist's style and costs more per square foot.

## Artwork specs that print sharp

- **Resolution:** at final size, 100–150 pixels per inch is usually enough for walls viewed from several feet away. Photography for close-up areas benefits from more.
- **Vector where possible:** logos and typography should be vector (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF) so they stay crisp at any size.
- **Colour:** supply brand colours as Pantone or CMYK values and ask for a printed colour proof on the actual material.
- **Bleed:** add at least 2–3 inches of extra image on every edge so installers can trim to uneven walls and ceilings.
- **Seams:** printed walls are hung in panels, typically 50–60 inches wide. Ask your designer to keep faces, logos and key text away from seam lines.
- **Obstacles:** mark outlets, switches, thermostats and door frames on the artwork template. Graphics are trimmed around them on site.

## Design ideas that work

1. **Origin story timeline** — founding date, first customer, key milestones. Great for corridors and recruiting.
2. **Oversized product macro** — one hero product photographed in extreme detail, wall-sized.
3. **Local love** — a stylised map or skyline of your city with your locations marked.
4. **Values in type** — three to five short statements set in your brand typeface at huge scale.
5. **Selfie wall** — a bold pattern plus your handle, sized for groups (around 8 feet wide).
6. **Customer faces** — with permission, real customers and their quotes; humanises service businesses.

## Installation tips

- Let new paint cure for at least a week (check the paint maker's guidance) before applying vinyl, or adhesive can pull paint off later.
- Avoid applying in very cold or humid conditions; adhesives have temperature ranges.
- Use a satin or eggshell paint base; flat matte paints can reduce adhesion for some vinyls.
- For exterior murals, choose materials and inks rated for UV exposure and your climate.
