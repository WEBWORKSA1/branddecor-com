Your reception logo is the handshake of your physical brand. It's photographed by visitors, appears behind staff in video calls and sets the tone for everything beyond the front desk. The good news: you don't need the most expensive finish to make it look premium. You need the right finish for your brand, the right size for the room, and decent lighting.

## The seven common finishes

### 1. Cut vinyl lettering
Flat lettering cut from adhesive vinyl and applied directly to the wall or glass. It's the fastest and cheapest option and can look sharp on a well-painted wall or frosted glass. It lacks depth, so it reads as "decal" rather than "sign" in larger spaces. Best for tight budgets, glass partitions and temporary offices.

### 2. 3D acrylic letters
Laser-cut acrylic, typically 3–25 mm thick, painted or in solid colour, mounted flush or on short standoffs. This is the default for good reason: it's crisp, comes in any colour, and the shadow it casts gives instant depth. Best for most offices, clinics and studios.

### 3. Laser-cut wood
Plywood, MDF or hardwood letters, natural or painted. Warm and tactile, ideal for cafés, creative studios and sustainability-led brands. Seal the wood properly, especially in kitchens or near entrances where humidity changes.

### 4. Brushed metal (aluminium or stainless)
Fabricated or cut metal letters with a brushed finish. Reads as architectural and permanent — a strong choice for law, finance, property and engineering firms. Fabricated letters with returns (sides) look more substantial than flat-cut plate.

### 5. Brass or gold finish
Solid brass or gold-finish metal for warmth and luxury. Works beautifully on dark walls, walnut slats and stone. Real brass patinas over time unless lacquered — decide whether that's a feature or a maintenance job.

### 6. LED neon flex
Flexible LED tubing shaped into your logo or a slogan, usually on a clear acrylic backer. Bright, social-media-friendly and much more durable and energy-efficient than glass neon. Best for hospitality, retail, creative and youth brands. Not every logo translates to a single-line neon form — expect a simplified version.

### 7. Halo-lit letters
Fabricated letters (often stainless) mounted off the wall with LEDs facing backwards, creating a soft glow behind each letter. The most premium look, and the most expensive: it needs electrical work, a transformer location and careful installation.

## How big should the logo be?

Start from the wall, not the logo file. Common guidance:

- **Width:** the logo should occupy roughly one-third to two-thirds of the visible wall width. Too small looks timid; edge-to-edge looks cramped.
- **Height on the wall:** centre the logo at or slightly above standing eye level (around 60–66 inches to the centre), higher if a reception desk sits in front of it.
- **Readability:** for any wording, allow around 1 inch of letter height for every 10 feet of viewing distance. Use our [letter height calculator](/tools.html#t-letter) for exact numbers.

## Mounting methods

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flush (VHB tape + silicone) | Adhered directly to the wall | Cheapest; fine for acrylic and light metal on smooth walls |
| Pin / stud mounted | Threaded pins set into drilled holes | Creates a shadow gap; needs a paper pattern for accurate drilling |
| Standoffs | Letters or a backing panel held off the wall on metal spacers | Very polished; ideal for logos on clear or coloured acrylic panels |
| Rail / panel | Logo mounted to a panel hung on a rail | Removable — useful in leased spaces |

## What drives the price?

1. **Size and letter count** — more letters and larger area mean more material and labour.
2. **Material and thickness** — vinyl &lt; acrylic &lt; wood &lt; metal &lt; illuminated.
3. **Complexity** — thin serifs, script fonts and tiny details increase fabrication time.
4. **Illumination** — adds LED modules, power supplies and an electrician.
5. **Wall type and access** — brick, concrete and double-height walls take longer to install.

## Lighting: the multiplier

Even an inexpensive acrylic logo looks premium when lit with a wash or spot from above at roughly 30 degrees. If your ceiling has track lighting, angle one head at the logo. If not, a slim linear LED wall-washer can usually be added for modest cost. Avoid lighting from directly in front — it flattens the letters and kills the shadow that gives depth.
