Visual merchandising is the craft of presenting products and space so shoppers notice, understand and buy. In-store branding is the frame around it: the colours, materials, signs and graphics that make the store unmistakably yours. Together they decide whether someone walks in, how long they stay and what they pick up.

## Follow the shopper's path

### 1. The window
Your window is a billboard with a three-second audience. Show one clear idea — a hero product, a story, a seasonal theme — rather than everything you sell. Change it regularly; returning passers-by stop noticing a window that never changes. Lighting is critical, especially after dark.

### 2. The decompression zone
The first several feet inside the door are where shoppers adjust to the new environment. Many retail designers keep this area relatively open and avoid placing important products or small signs here, because they're often missed. Place your first strong display just beyond it.

### 3. The power wall
Many shoppers drift to one side after entering — in many markets, to the right. The first wall they face is prime space for your brand statement and best-selling or highest-margin products. Observe your own store's traffic to confirm which way your customers turn.

### 4. Sightlines and the back wall
Strong colour, a bold graphic or lighting on the back wall draws people deeper into the store. Keep fixtures low enough near the front that shoppers can see it.

### 5. The checkout
Queues are dwell time. Use them for impulse items, loyalty sign-up, reviews and a branded "thank you" moment.

## Signage hierarchy

Good stores use a clear hierarchy so signs don't compete:

1. **Brand** — the logo and big statement, seen from the door.
2. **Department / category** — tells people where things are.
3. **Feature / promotion** — highlights a story or offer on a display.
4. **Product / price** — the detail at the point of decision.

Keep fonts and colours consistent across all four levels. Size each level for its viewing distance; our [letter height calculator](/tools.html#t-letter) helps.

## Display principles

- **The rule of three.** Odd-numbered groupings, especially triangles of three heights, look natural and draw the eye.
- **Colour blocking.** Grouping products by colour creates impact from a distance.
- **Space is luxury.** Fewer products, more space reads as higher value.
- **Touch and try.** Displays that invite handling sell better than products behind glass, where appropriate.
- **Tell a story.** Group products by use or occasion ("weekend at the lake") rather than only by category.

## Lighting

Layer three types: ambient (overall), accent (on displays and walls — often brighter than ambient to create contrast) and task (checkout, fitting rooms). Warmer colour temperatures feel cosy and suit food and lifestyle brands; cooler, brighter light suits tech and pharmacies. Make sure colours on product and graphics render accurately under your lights.

## Common mistakes

- Too many small signs competing for attention.
- Hand-written or dated promotional signs left up after the offer ends.
- Branding only the façade — the interior feels generic.
- Displays that block sightlines to the back of the store.
- Ignoring fitting rooms and washrooms, which shoppers remember.
