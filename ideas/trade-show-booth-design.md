Trade shows are expensive, crowded and loud. Attendees walk aisles scanning hundreds of booths, giving each one a few seconds. A good booth is designed around that reality: a message readable from the aisle, an inviting layout and a clear path from "hello" to a qualified lead.

## Booth types at a glance

| Type | Open sides | Typical size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inline (linear) | 1 | 10×10, 10×20 | Most common; back wall does the heavy lifting |
| Corner | 2 | 10×10, 10×20 | Extra visibility from a second aisle |
| Peninsula | 3 | 20×20+ | Back wall shared with another booth |
| Island | 4 | 20×20+ | Visible from all sides; allows height and hanging signs |

Show rules usually limit height and how much of an inline booth's sides can be enclosed — read the exhibitor manual before design starts.

## The three-second headline

Your back wall's top third is what people see over the crowd. Put a short benefit headline there — ideally seven words or fewer — plus your logo. "We make X faster" beats your company name alone. Keep detailed copy lower, at eye level, for people already in the booth.

## Layout zones

1. **Attract (aisle edge):** keep it open. A table across the front blocks visitors; move it to the side or back.
2. **Engage (middle):** demo station, product display, touchscreen or interactive experience.
3. **Convert (back or side):** a quieter spot for conversations, with lead capture (badge scanner, tablet form or QR code) right there.

## Graphics and materials

- **Fabric graphics (SEG / tension)** are lightweight, wrinkle-resistant and ship compactly — the modern default.
- **Backlit fabric** stands out dramatically under dim hall lighting.
- **Modular frames** let one kit reconfigure from 10×10 to 10×20 across shows.
- **Floor:** branded carpet or interlocking flooring defines your space and is kinder to staff feet.

## Lighting

Convention halls are often poorly lit. Overhead arm lights or LED strips on graphics, and backlighting where possible, make a dramatic difference. Budget for electrical services from the venue.

## Budgeting

Floor space is the biggest single cost and often runs around 20–35% of a total show budget. Industry rules of thumb suggest multiplying your space cost by roughly three or four to estimate the full cost of exhibiting, once you include the exhibit itself, shipping, drayage, labour, electrical, travel and promotion. Exhibit cost can be spread across several shows if you reuse a modular kit.

Model your show with the [booth budget and ROI calculator](/tools.html#t-booth).

## Measuring ROI

Track three numbers for every show:

- **Cost per lead** = total show cost ÷ leads captured.
- **Qualified lead rate** — what share actually fit your buyer profile.
- **Pipeline and closed revenue** attributed to show leads within 3–12 months.

If cost per lead is far higher than your digital channels, improve qualification and follow-up before cutting the show — most trade-show value is lost after the event, in slow follow-up.

## Pre-show checklist

- Read the exhibitor manual: height limits, deadlines, fire-rating rules.
- Order graphics with time for a proof and a test build.
- Book electrical, internet and lead-retrieval early — late orders cost more.
- Train staff: greeting line, qualifying questions, hand-off to the convert zone.
- Plan follow-up emails before you leave for the show.
