An office is the only marketing channel your employees experience every working day. Visitors judge it in seconds; candidates use it to decide whether they'd fit in. Yet most offices are branded in one place only — a logo behind reception — and everything beyond the front door feels like any other rented floor.

This guide walks through every zone of a typical office, what works in each, and roughly what it costs, so you can build a plan that fits your budget instead of a wish list.

## Start with three questions {#start}

**1. Who needs to feel what?** Clients in reception need confidence. Employees in work zones need focus and belonging. Candidates on tour need to see culture. Write one sentence for each audience — it will drive every design decision.

**2. What is fixed?** Leased spaces limit drilling, painting and permanent fixtures. Ask your landlord what's allowed, and favour removable wallcovering, static-cling film and standoff-mounted panels if you plan to move within five years.

**3. What's the one message?** Pick one idea your space should communicate — "precision", "warmth", "we build things", "you're in safe hands". A single idea keeps a dozen installations from feeling like a dozen different brands.

## Zone-by-zone ideas {#zones}

### Reception and entry

This is where most budget should go per square foot. A dimensional logo is the anchor; lighting is what makes it look expensive. A brushed-metal or acrylic logo with a wash light from above reads as premium even in a modest space. Add a short brand statement or a client-facing proof point (years in business, projects delivered) at eye level — not a wall of text. See our [reception logo wall guide](/ideas/reception-logo-wall-ideas.html) for material comparisons.

### Corridors and circulation

Corridors are long, empty canvases people walk past many times a day. Use them for story: a company timeline, project photography, a map of clients or offices, or oversized typography of your values. Printed wallcovering is the workhorse here — it's durable, cleanable and cost-effective on long runs.

### Meeting rooms

Glass-walled rooms need manifestation (visible markings on glass) for safety in many building codes, so make it branded: a frosted band with your logo pattern cut out, or a gradient film that gives privacy at seated height. Name rooms after something meaningful to your brand — products, cities, founders' heroes — and put the name on the door in your brand typeface.

### Work zones

Keep it calm. Workspaces need focus, so use brand colour sparingly — on acoustic panels, a single feature wall, or zone markers that double as wayfinding ("North Studio", "Team Atlas"). Acoustic panels printed or upholstered in brand colours solve two problems at once.

### Kitchen and social areas

This is where culture shows. Murals, a values wall, a photo wall of team events, or a chalkboard wall for shout-outs make people linger. Neon-style LED signs with internal jokes or slogans are popular and photograph well for recruiting posts.

### Exterior and windows

Don't forget the street. Window vinyl with your logo and hours, a projecting blade sign, or door decals are the first touchpoint for walk-ins and delivery drivers. Exterior signs usually need a permit — your sign maker should handle it.

## Typical budgets {#budget}

Planning ranges vary by city and supplier, but these bands help set expectations for a 3,000–5,000 sq ft office:

| Level | What it includes | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Reception logo (acrylic or vinyl), meeting-room film, door names | $3,000 – $8,000 |
| Standard | Metal reception logo with lighting, 3–5 graphic walls, branded film, wayfinding | $8,000 – $25,000 |
| Immersive | Feature walls in every zone, custom fabrication, floors, ceilings, digital | $25,000+ |

## Timeline {#timeline}

1. **Brief and site survey (week 1).** Measure walls, photograph each zone, gather brand files (vector logo, fonts, colour codes).
2. **Concepts (weeks 2–3).** Expect two rounds of design revisions.
3. **Proofs and samples (week 4).** Approve colour on the actual material — screens lie.
4. **Production (weeks 5–6).** Printed graphics take days; fabricated metal signs can take three weeks or more.
5. **Installation (week 7).** Usually done after hours or over a weekend. Fresh paint needs at least a week to cure before wall graphics go on.

## Mistakes to avoid {#mistakes}

- **Too many messages.** Seven slogans on seven walls equals no message. Pick one idea and three proof points.
- **Ignoring lighting.** A beautiful logo in a dark corner is invisible. Budget lighting with the sign.
- **Low-resolution artwork.** Large-format prints need vector logos and high-resolution photography — check files before design starts.
- **Branding the wrong walls.** Stand where visitors actually stand. The wall behind a door that's always open is wasted.
- **Forgetting the camera.** Many visitors now see your office first on video calls and social posts. Brand the walls that appear in those frames.

<div class="callout"><b>Quick win:</b> If you only do one thing this quarter, light your existing reception logo properly and add branded film to one glass wall. It's the highest impact per dollar in most offices.</div>
